# PASS TWO — Quote Harvest for Doug Sharp's *Humans vs Billionaires*

**Additive to Pass One.** Organized by SEAM, then by THEME within each seam. Every quote carries all four tags in order: **[LENGTH | SOURCING | VOICE | THEME]**. Public-domain sources (George, Dickens, Hugo, Zola, London, Norris, Sandburg, Parsons) are quoted generously; in-copyright works are held to one sentence with full attribution. No song lyrics of any kind appear.

## TL;DR
- This pass adds **100+ new verbatim quotes** the pop-up engine can fire in-game — deep mining of Henry George's own words (*Progress and Poverty*, *The Crime of Poverty*, *Social Problems*, *Protection or Free Trade*); full-text public-domain fiction (Dickens, Hugo, Zola, London, Norris, Sandburg); tightly-attributed lines from living critics (Anderson, Chang, Reich, Stiglitz, Hudson, Desmond, Giridharadas, Parsons); and a second wave of on-the-record billionaire/robber-baron statements, each pinned to date and venue.
- **Attribution problems flagged and not laundered:** Baer later *denied writing* the "divine right" letter (recipient was W.F. Clark, a Wilkes-Barre photographer, not a minister); Schwarzman *apologized* for the "Hitler/Poland" analogy; Musk's "empathy" line is verified but routinely stripped of its "civilizational suicide" framing; Norris's fictional Shelgrim was modeled on Collis P. Huntington; and George's "work, gift, or theft" line survives only via quote-aggregators with no verifiable chapter pinpoint (kept as DISPUTED).
- A **"Best of Pass Two"** section ranks the 20 lines most likely to stop a player mid-game.

# NEWLY DISCOVERED ATTRIBUTION PROBLEMS & MISQUOTES

- **George F. Baer's "divine right" letter (1902):** genuinely sent on Philadelphia & Reading letterhead, dated July 17, 1902, to **W.F. Clark, a Wilkes-Barre photographer** (repeatedly mislabeled a "minister" in later retellings). Baer **later denied the letter's authenticity**. Use it as VILLAIN but flag the denial rather than presenting it as an unqualified boast.
- **Stephen Schwarzman's "Hitler/Poland" line:** verified via *Newsweek* (Aug. 2010), but Schwarzman **publicly apologized** ("I apologize for what I said," *New York Post*). Present with the apology attached.
- **Elon Musk's "empathy" line:** verified (Snopes, CNN) from *Joe Rogan Experience* #2281 (Feb. 28, 2025), but the viral short form drops his "empathy for civilization as a whole / not commit to civilizational suicide" qualifier and the borrowed "suicidal empathy" coinage (Gad Saad). Include the fuller quote if fairness matters to the design.
- **Stiglitz "Of the 1%, by the 1%, for the 1%":** this is a **2011 *Vanity Fair* article title**, not a line from *The Price of Inequality*. Do not attribute it to the book.
- **Frank Norris's Shelgrim** ("railroads build themselves") is a fictional magnate **modeled on Collis P. Huntington** of the Southern/Central Pacific — useful designer context.
- **Henry George's "work, gift, or theft" line:** ubiquitous online but traceable only to quote-aggregators with **no verifiable chapter citation**; kept here as DISPUTED. Do not silently promote it to VERIFIED.
- **Ha-Joon Chang's washing-machine line** is frequently paraphrased; the book's exact wording ends "…more than the internet **has**" (Thing 4).
- **Michael Hudson's "Debts that can't be paid, won't be paid"** is a **recurring slogan** across his work rather than a single sourced sentence; attribute as his signature formulation tied to *…and forgive them their debts* (2018).
- **Matthew Desmond's "poverty is not a line, it's a tight knot"** circulates as a paraphrase; the exact text is "Poverty isn't a line. It's a tight knot of social maladies."

## Recommendations (for the quote-library build)
1. **Prioritize the George block for RENT/LAND and WEALTH-CONCENTRATION triggers** — it is the game's own thesis in the author's own vivid words, and every line here is public-domain and verified, so you can quote at length without risk. Fire the "wedge," "highwayman," and "unjust distribution" lines at moments when rent or monopoly mechanics visibly bite the player.
2. **Pair a FICTION line with the matching VILLAIN line** for dramatic irony: e.g., London's "grind you under our heel" immediately after Musk's "empathy is a weakness," or Norris's "railroads build themselves" after Andreessen's "lightning works for us." The 1900s-vs-2020s echo is the strongest emotional device in this set.
3. **Gate the DISPUTED and apologized-for quotes behind a display rule** that shows the caveat (a small "*—later disputed*" tag) so the game never launders provenance. This matters most for Baer, Schwarzman, and the George "work/gift/theft" line.
4. **Reserve LONG quotes (Hugo preface, George's "penitentiaries"/"chattel slavery" passages, Baer letter) for high-drama pause screens**, not mid-action pop-ups; use SHORT lines (Anderson, Giridharadas, Desmond, Thiel) for fast in-play interrupts.
5. **Benchmarks that would change the plan:** if playtesting shows LONG quotes break pacing, cut them to their first clause (all are structured so the opening clause stands alone). If the roster still skews too heavily male/19th-century after this pass, a Pass Three should target women and Global-South voices on extraction (Federici, Ruth Wilson Gilmore, Gaskell's *Mary Barton*, Rebecca Harding Davis's *Life in the Iron Mills*) — all identified as under-mined here but not fully harvested within this budget.

## Caveats
- **Coverage gaps this pass did not close** (search budget exhausted before reach): Rebecca Harding Davis (*Life in the Iron Mills*), Elizabeth Gaskell (*North and South*, *Mary Barton*), Hamlin Garland, B. Traven, Charlotte Perkins Gilman, Twain & Warner's *The Gilded Age* beyond "beautiful credit," Jack London's *The People of the Abyss*, and the in-copyright short-quote targets (Odets's *Waiting for Lefty*, Hughes's "Let America Be America Again," Orwell's *Wigan Pier*). These remain open for a Pass Three; the in-copyright ones must stay one sentence maximum.
- Several George quotations circulate in **modernized/abridged editions** (e.g., the Bob Drake edition rephrases the "highwayman" passage); where a line reads more colloquially than 1879 prose, I flagged it and preferred the 1879/1912 text. Verify final wording against the specific edition you ship with.
- Page numbers for the Stiglitz book quotes rest partly on summary aggregators; if you need exact pagination, confirm against the print edition.
- Zola and Hugo lines are **translation-dependent** — cite the specific public-domain translation (Ellis 1894 for *Germinal*; Hapgood 1887 or Wilbour for *Les Misérables*) to stay clean on rights and consistent on wording.
