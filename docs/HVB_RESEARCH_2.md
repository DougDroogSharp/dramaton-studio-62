# CHAPTER 1 — WILLIAM THE CONQUEROR (1066–1087)

## METER-MOVING INCIDENTS & DECISIONS

- **The papal banner (1066) — PRESTIGE ↑, REGULATION ↓ (moral-legal cover for extraction).** Pope Alexander II sent William a consecrated banner (*vexillum*) and blessing, converting a land-grab into holy war. Justification: Harold Godwinson's alleged broken oath. Lanfranc argued the case at Rome; Archdeacon Hildebrand (the future Gregory VII) pushed the favorable decision, with Harold arraigned as "an oath-breaker and a violator of sacred relics." *Sources: English Heritage; C. Morton, "Pope Alexander II and the Norman Conquest," Latomus 34 (1975), 362–82.* (Note: the banner's date is debated — some argue it was formally conferred only at a later Winchester ceremony c.1070.)
- **Land-for-swords recruitment (Council of Lillebonne, c. Jan 1066) — GREED / HIERARCHY / RENT.** Beyond his Normandy and Maine troops, William drew mercenaries and adventurers from Brittany, Flanders, and France, "offering promises of English lands and titles." This is the Georgist seed of the whole chapter: conquest → property → rent, in perpetuity.
- **Fleet assembly at Dives-sur-Mer — SPECULATION / HOARD.** The fleet gathered at the mouth of the Dives ~12 Aug 1066, was windbound about a month, shifted to St-Valéry, and crossed the night of 27–28 Sept, landing at Pevensey. The near-contemporary *Ship List of William the Conqueror* records ~776 vessels pledged (Odo of Bayeux 100, Robert of Mortain 120); Wace later gives 696. Modern army estimates cluster at 7,000–14,000 men. *Sources: English Heritage; the Ship List (c.1130–60 MS); Wace, Roman de Rou.*
- **Harrying of the North (winter 1069–70) — REPRESSION spike, FLAREUPS, CRISIS.** After northern and Danish-backed rebellions, William laid waste Yorkshire by scorched earth. Orderic Vitalis states "more than 100,000 people perished of hunger." Domesday (1086) still records vast Yorkshire "wasteland" two decades later. **FLAG (contested number):** historians treat 100,000 as rhetorical — Orderic wrote ~55 years later — but agree it records a genuine, exceptional atrocity (Mark Hagger argues it was "no worse than other similar conflicts of the time"; most modern scholars disagree).
- **Domesday Book (1086) — the ledger of captured rent.** The systematic survey of who holds what: conquest crystallized into permanent, inheritable property and rent — the mechanical heart of the chapter.

## CUT-SCENE DIALOGUE

- **Orderic on the Harrying** *(reported/literary; written c.1114–41, ~55 yrs after):* "The King stopped at nothing to hunt his enemies… To his shame, William made no effort to control his fury, punishing the innocent with the guilty. He ordered that crops and herds, tools and food be burned to ashes. More than 100,000 people perished of hunger. I have often praised William in this book, but I can say nothing good about this brutal slaughter. God will punish him." *[Orderic Vitalis, Ecclesiastical History, Bk IV]*
- **William's deathbed confession** *(FLAG: Orderic's literary/moralizing reconstruction — not a transcript; the near-contemporary De Obitu Willelmi is far briefer):* "I have persecuted the natives of England beyond all reason. Whether gentle or simple I have cruelly oppressed them; many I unjustly disinherited; innumerable multitudes perished through me by famine or the sword… I fell on the English of the northern shires like a ravening lion… Having gained the throne of that kingdom by so many crimes I dare not leave it to anyone but God." *[Orderic Vitalis, Historia Ecclesiastica]*
- **Harold's oath at Bayeux** *(FLAG: Norman propaganda; no English corroboration):* per Orderic, "Harold himself had taken an oath of fealty to Duke William at Rouen… and after becoming his man had sworn on the most sacred relics to carry out all that was required of him." Depicted in the Bayeux Tapestry. The tradition that the relics were *hidden* beneath the table (so Harold swore unknowingly) is itself a Norman narrative device.
- **Coronation fire, 25 Dec 1066** *(reported by Orderic):* Norman guards outside Westminster mistook the roar of bilingual acclamation for an assassination attempt and set fire to surrounding houses; the congregation fled in panic while, per Orderic, only a few clergy remained to finish the rite as William "trembled" amid the flames.

## SECONDARY FIGURES

- **Odo of Bayeux** (half-brother via Herleva): Bishop of Bayeux, **Earl of Kent**; supplied 100 ships; per canon law wielded a mace at Hastings; likely commissioned the Bayeux Tapestry; Domesday lands valued ~£3,000 across ~22 counties. Later arrested and tried by William for defrauding the crown; died 1097 en route to Crusade.
- **Robert of Mortain** (half-brother): **Count of Mortain, Earl of Cornwall**; 120 ships; ~797 manors — **the largest lay landholder in England after the king**.
- **William fitzOsbern**: boyhood friend and cousin; **Earl of Hereford**, given the Isle of Wight; co-regent of England; killed at the Battle of Cassel, 1071.
- **Roger de Montgomery**: **Earl of Shrewsbury and Arundel**; near-palatine earldom on the Welsh border; married Mabel de Bellême.
- **Alan Rufus (Alan the Red of Brittany)**: Breton leader; granted the vast **Honour of Richmond** (built Richmond Castle from 1071), much of it seized from Harold's widow Edith the Fair; appears in ~1,017 Domesday entries — third after the king and Robert of Mortain.
- **Geoffrey de Mandeville**: among the ten richest magnates; first Norman sheriff of London and Middlesex; built Pleshey, Essex; lands taken largely from the dispossessed Englishman Esgar/Ansgar the Staller.
- **Archbishop Lanfranc** (Bec/Caen): **Archbishop of Canterbury from 1070**; enforced church reform, established Canterbury's primacy over York (Accord of Winchester, 1072), and rebuilt Canterbury Cathedral on the model of St-Étienne, Caen.
- **Ealdred, Archbishop of York**: **crowned William at Westminster, 25 Dec 1066** (having earlier crowned Harold); addressed the English in English while Geoffrey of Coutances addressed the Normans in French. Died 1069.

## THE PRESTIGE MACHINE

- **Penitential Ordinance of Ermenfrid of Sion (c.1070)** — penance imposed on the *victors*: "Anyone who knows that he killed a man in the great battle must do penance for one year for each man that he killed"; wounding = 40 days each; archers (whose kills were uncountable) = "three Lents." Restitution required for church plunder. **Notably, Duke William himself is unmentioned/exempt** — Freeman noted that by the ordinance's own logic William would owe ~2,000 years of penance. *Definitive study: H.E.J. Cowdrey, Journal of Ecclesiastical History 20 (1969), 225–42.*
- **Battle Abbey**: founded c.1070 on the Hastings battlefield with the **high altar deliberately placed on the spot where Harold died** (per the *Chronicle of Battle Abbey* and William of Malmesbury) — simultaneously victory monument and papal-ordered penance; consecrated 1094.
- **Church-building boom / Lanfranc patronage**: Norman Romanesque cathedrals (Canterbury, St Albans, Winchester, York, later Durham); the Caen twin abbeys (St-Étienne / Abbaye aux Hommes and La Trinité / Abbaye aux Dames), originally founded to secure papal dispensation for William and Matilda's consanguineous marriage.
- **Gesta Guillelmi** by William of Poitiers — William's own chaplain — is overt court propaganda casting Harold as perjurer/usurper and William as divinely favored heir. Standard scholarship (Bates, Morris, van Houts) reads it as such.

## CHAPTER-SPECIFIC DEEP FEATURE: FORCE-AGGREGATION → LAND DYNASTIES

The Georgist through-line — 1066 conquest converted into hereditary property still yielding rent nearly a millennium later:

- **Grosvenor family / Dukes of Westminster**: trace descent from Gilbert le Grosveneur ("chief huntsman"), a relative of William and nephew of Hugh Lupus, Earl of Chester, who came in 1066 and received Cheshire lands. The family later acquired ~500 acres of Mayfair/Belgravia via the 1677 marriage of Sir Thomas Grosvenor and Mary Davies, and still owns much of it. **Hugh Grosvenor, 7th Duke of Westminster, ranked 14th on the Sunday Times Rich List 2025 at an estimated £9.884 billion** (the Grosvenor family holding), down from £10.127bn in 2024 — the second-largest North-West fortune behind Sir Jim Ratcliffe (£17.046bn). *Source: Sunday Times Rich List 2025 (via Chester Standard).*
- **Fitzalan-Howard / Dukes of Norfolk**: hold Arundel Castle and the hereditary office of Earl Marshal; descend via multiple Norman lines (Bigod, d'Aubigny). Current head: Edward Fitzalan-Howard, 18th Duke of Norfolk.
- **Alan Rufus's fortune, quantified**: Philip Beresford & William D. Rubinstein, *The Richest of the Rich* (Harriman House, 2007), calculate his ~£11,000 fortune at his 1093 death as **7.33% of English net national income — equivalent to ~£81 billion today — making him the wealthiest Briton in history**. Beresford: "No-one is likely to be as rich as Alan Rufus again." (Popular-media figures of ~$178–182bn are looser extrapolations; use the Beresford/Rubinstein figure.)
- **Surname-persistence evidence (Gregory Clark & Neil Cummins, LSE/UC Davis, 2013–14)**: Norman-origin surnames (Darcy, Baskerville, Mandeville, Montgomery, Percy) remain statistically overrepresented at Oxbridge nearly 1,000 years on, with an intergenerational status-persistence coefficient of **b ≈ 0.9** for the Norman sample. Cummins: "Surnames such as Baskerville, Darcy, Mandeville and Montgomery are still over-represented at Oxbridge and also among elite occupations such as medicine, law and politics." Norman names were ~1,500% overrepresented at Oxbridge in 1215 and still ~25–28% overrepresented in 2011 — the game-mechanic proof that captured land became captured status for 27+ generations.

# CHAPTER 2 — KING LEOPOLD II / CONGO FREE STATE (1885–1908)

## METER-MOVING INCIDENTS & DECISIONS

- **Berlin Conference (1884–85) — HIERARCHY / REGULATION ↓.** International recognition of the Congo as Leopold's *personal* domain, distinct from the Belgian state.
- **Rubber quota system — GREED / RENT.** In districts such as Bongandanga, villagers owed roughly 3 kg of dried rubber per man per fortnight, verified by baskets; collecting it forced them to abandon subsistence farming.
- **The "red rubber" margin — GREED / HOARD.** ABIR "enjoyed a boom through the late 1890s, by selling a kilogram of rubber in Europe that cost them just 1.35 francs for up to 10 francs." The financial escalation was staggering: ABIR shares rose from 500 gold francs (1892) to 15,000 (1903), and the annual dividend from 1 franc (1892) to 1,200 francs (1903); Leopold personally netted an estimated 70 million Belgian francs from the Congo between 1896 and 1905. *Sources: Lumen Learning/History Guild; Wikipedia, "Congo Free State propaganda war" and "Atrocities in the Congo Free State."*
- **Hostage-houses (maisons d'otages) — REPRESSION.** Women and children were held to force men to meet quotas; agents later imprisoned chiefs directly — "in July 1902 one post recorded that it held 44 chiefs in prison," and "the posts at Bongandanga and Mompono each recorded death rates of three to ten prisoners per day in 1899." *Source: Wikipedia, "Atrocities in the Congo Free State," and "Abir Congo Company."*
- **The chicotte and severed-hands system — REPRESSION / terror.** A whip of raw, sun-dried hippopotamus hide; Force Publique soldiers were required to bring severed hands as proof they hadn't wasted cartridges.
- **Casement Report (1904) — EDUCATION spike.** British consul Roger Casement documented mutilations, killings, and hostage-taking with names, dates, and places; it catalyzed the Congo Reform Association.
- **1908 annexation by the Belgian parliament — REGULATION finally bites.** Leopold was forced to relinquish the Free State.

## CUT-SCENE DIALOGUE

- **Refugee to Casement** *(verbatim, Casement Report):* "They killed my little sister, threw her in a house, and set it on fire."
- **Witness "U.U." to Casement** *(verbatim):* "As we fled, the soldiers killed ten children, in the water. They killed a lot of adults, cut off their hands, put them in baskets, and took them to the white man, who counted 200 hands…"
- **Tswambe on Léon Fiévez** *(reported by a Catholic priest, quoted in Adam Hochschild, King Leopold's Ghost):* "All blacks saw this man as the devil of the Equator… From all the bodies killed in the field, you had to cut off the hands. He wanted to see the number of hands cut off by each soldier, who had to bring them in baskets… A village which refused to provide rubber would be completely swept clean."
- **Charles Lemaire, former Equateur commissioner** *(recalled after retirement, quoted in Hochschild):* "As soon as it was a question of rubber, I wrote to the government, 'To gather rubber in the district… one must cut off hands, noses and ears.'"
- **Casement witness with the knotted string** *(via Arthur Conan Doyle, The Crime of the Congo):* a witness "appeared with a string knotted in forty-two places, and with a packet of fifty leaves" — each knot a murder, each leaf a rope in his village.

## SECONDARY FIGURES

- **Léon Fiévez**: Equateur District commissioner from 1 April 1893; "the devil of the Equator"; in late 1894 alone his forces burned/looted over 160 villages. Foreign-press coverage forced his removal.
- **Charles Lemaire**: earlier Equateur commissioner (1890–93); author of the explicit hand-cutting order above; later first director of Belgium's Colonial University.
- **Léon Rom**: Force Publique officer whose atrocities have led some to see him as a model for Conrad's Kurtz.
- **Roger Casement**: British consul, author of the 1904 report; later an Irish nationalist executed in 1916.
- **E.D. Morel**: Liverpool shipping clerk turned campaigner; founded the Congo Reform Association (1904).
- **Alice Seeley Harris**: missionary photographer who in 1904 photographed Nsala of Wala staring at the severed hand and foot of his five-year-old daughter Boali — an image E.D. Morel published in *King Leopold's Rule in Africa*.
- **ABIR (Anglo-Belgian India Rubber Company)**: the concession company running quota terror in the Maringa-Lopori basin.

## THE PRESTIGE MACHINE — "The Builder King"

- **1897 Brussels International Exposition / Tervuren human zoo**: Leopold had 267 Congolese brought to Tervuren and displayed behind a fence; **seven died** — Ekia, Gemba, Kitukwa, Mpela, Zao, Samba, and Mbange — of pneumonia and influenza in July–August 1897, and were buried by the parish church of Sint-Jan Evangelist. When crowds tossed the captives candy and they sickened, Leopold reportedly posted the equivalent of a "the blacks are fed by the organizing committee" notice. *Sources: AfricaMuseum (Royal Museum for Central Africa); Hochschild.*
- **Royal Museum for Central Africa (Tervuren)**: grew out of the 1897 colonial exhibition; the neoclassical Africa Palace was built by Leopold's order.
- **Monumental building program**: the Cinquantenaire Arch, the Royal Galleries of Ostend, and Antwerp works — substantially funded by Congo profits routed through the Crown Foundation (Fondation de la Couronne).
- **Press bribery**: Leopold ran a paid international press operation; the Kowalsky affair (1906) exposed his U.S. lobbyist's papers in the *New York American*.

## CHAPTER-SPECIFIC DEEP FEATURE: THE SLAVERY SYSTEM MECHANICS

- **Force Publique**: a private army with white officers and conscripts from across the Congo, some kidnapped as children and raised in mission-military conditions "close to slavery."
- **Quota arithmetic**: the ~1.35-fr/kg-cost-to-10-fr/kg-sale "red rubber" margin (and the share/dividend explosion above) is the extraction engine to model directly.
- **Hostage houses, chain gangs, porterage, railway toll**: deaths on the Matadi–Léopoldville caravan and railway routes; the *colonies scolaires* conscripting raid-orphaned children.
- **Death toll (FLAG: contested range)**: modern estimates run from **1.2 million to 10 million**; Hochschild popularized ~10 million (~50% of the population) — a genuinely disputed figure combining killing, forced-labor death, starvation, disease, and falling birth rates.
- **Irony to foreground**: Leopold's anti-slavery Brussels Conference rhetoric (1889–90) set against the forced-labor reality — prestige language as insulation for the extraction it concealed.

# CHAPTER 3 — AL CAPONE / KING OF CHICAGO (1920–1931)

## METER-MOVING INCIDENTS & DECISIONS

- **Cicero takeover (1924 election) — RENT / REPRESSION.** ~200 armed Capone men manned the polls; Chicago Crime Commission director Virgil Peterson called it "one of the most disgraceful episodes in American municipal history." Capone's brother Frank was killed by police that day. Result: captured territorial rent over the suburb.
- **Beer economics — GREED / HOARD.** "His syndicate's net profits in the late 1920's were estimated by the Chicago Crime Commission at sixty million dollars a year" *(American Heritage, John G. Mitchell, Feb 1979)*; broader revenue estimates reached ~$100 million.
- **St. Valentine's Day Massacre (14 Feb 1929) — REPRESSION spike that SHATTERED PRESTIGE.** Seven of Bugs Moran's North Siders were gunned down; the public revulsion turned the tide.
- **Chicago Crime Commission Public Enemies List (24 April 1930) — EDUCATION.** 28 names; Al Capone was #1.
- **Net-worth tax prosecution — REGULATION innovation.** Treasury's Elmer Irey and investigator Frank Wilson built the case; bookkeepers Leslie Shumway and Fred Reis flipped and were placed under protection.
- **Soup kitchen (Nov 1930) — PRESTIGE counter-move** as the tax net closed.

## CUT-SCENE DIALOGUE

- **Frank Gusenberg to Sgt. Clarence Sweeney** *(reported; FLAG: probably apocryphal per some historians — Gusenberg had been shot 14 times and died ~3 hours later):* "No one… nobody shot me."
- **Judge James Wilkerson rejecting the plea bargain** *(verbatim, court record, 30 July 1931):* "It is time for somebody to impress upon the defendant that it is utterly impossible to bargain with a Federal court." And: "The parties to a criminal case may not stipulate as to the judgment to be entered."
- **Capone to reporters before his expected sentencing** *(verbatim, widely reported):* "I've been made an issue and I'm not complaining, but why don't they go after all those bankers who took the savings of thousands of poor people and lost them in bank failures?"
- **Capone associate on the soup kitchen** *(quoted in a Chicago newspaper):* "He couldn't stand it to see those poor devils starving, and nobody else seemed to be doing much, so the big boy decided to do it himself."
- **Attributed to Capone** *(FLAG: attributed/apocryphal, per National Archives):* "The income tax law is a lot of bunk. The government can't collect legal taxes from illegal money."

## SECONDARY FIGURES

- **Frank Nitti** ("The Enforcer") and **Jake "Greasy Thumb" Guzik**: top lieutenants, both tax-convicted (1930) as a prelude to Capone.
- **Ralph "Bottles" Capone**: brother, tax-convicted 1930.
- **Judge James H. Wilkerson**: rejected the plea deal; sentenced Capone on 24 Oct 1931 to 11 years, a $50,000 fine, plus $215,000 back taxes.
- **George E.Q. Johnson**: U.S. Attorney / chief prosecutor.
- **Elmer Irey**: head of the Treasury Intelligence Unit (probe began 18 Oct 1928); **Frank Wilson**: lead investigator whose 1933 report reconstructed Capone's cash operation.
- **Leslie Shumway & Fred Reis**: Capone bookkeepers turned protected witnesses.
- **Frank & Peter Gusenberg**: North Side enforcers, massacre victims; **Jack "Machine Gun" McGurn**: suspected massacre planner.
- **The jury**: the names, addresses, and occupations of the 12 jurors were printed in Chicago newspapers (per the National Archives).

## THE PRESTIGE MACHINE

- **Soup kitchen at 935 S. State St.**: opened mid-November 1930, serving "an average of 2,200 Chicagoans every day" *(HISTORY.com)*; a Chicago Tribune headline read "120,000 meals are served by Capone Free Soup Kitchen"; on Thanksgiving 1930 it fed ~5,000. It closed in April 1932 even as national unemployment kept rising — the PR utility exhausted.
- **Cultivated press persona**: Capone courted reporters as strategy; the Hearst papers and Damon Runyon-era columnists lionized him; he styled himself a "public benefactor"/Robin Hood.
- **Milk-for-schoolchildren legend** *(FLAG: disputed/apocryphal)*: the chocolate-milk-dating story is popular but unverified.

## CHAPTER-SPECIFIC DEEP FEATURE: ST. VALENTINE'S DAY MASSACRE ↔ MODEL & RACKET-AS-RENT

- **Model mapping**: the massacre is the moment REPRESSION spiked so high it destroyed PRESTIGE and triggered REGULATION — public revulsion → the "Public Enemy" list → federal "get Capone" drive → the net-worth tax prosecution. It is the beginning of the end.
- **Racket-as-rent mechanics**: protection-racket pricing; the ~$60M/year beer economy; Cicero as captured territorial rent; systematic bribery (Mayor "Big Bill" Thompson kept a picture of Capone; graft ran down through aldermen and police); union/labor racketeering.
- **Regulation innovation**: the net-worth/tax-evasion method — capturing extraction the traditional criminal law couldn't reach — is the regulatory "unlock" of the chapter.

# CHAPTER 4 — ELON MUSK (2020s)

## METER-MOVING INCIDENTS & DECISIONS

- **"Funding secured" tweet (7 Aug 2018) & SEC settlement — REGULATION vs SPECULATION.** "Am considering taking Tesla private at $420. Funding secured." Tesla stock spiked ~14%. The SEC charged securities fraud; Musk and Tesla each paid $20M, Musk stepped down as chairman for three years, and a "Twitter sitter" pre-approval regime was imposed. (A 2023 shareholder class action later found him *not* liable for the tweets.)
- **Twitter acquisition ($44B, 2022) — HOARD / SPECULATION**, financed largely with Tesla stock.
- **Tornetta v. Musk (30 Jan 2024) — REGULATION strike.** Chancellor Kathaleen McCormick voided Musk's ~$56B (est. $55.8B) 2018 pay package — 33× the next-largest executive package — as an "unfathomable sum," and upheld the decision again in December 2024 after a shareholder re-vote.
- **Delaware → Texas/Nevada reincorporation — REGULATION corrosion/escape.** Musk moved Tesla's state of incorporation out of Delaware; "Never incorporate your company in the state of Delaware."
- **SpaceX worker injuries — GREED / REPRESSION of safety.** A Reuters Special Report (10 Nov 2023), "At SpaceX, worker injuries soar in Elon Musk's rush to Mars," documented **at least 600 previously unreported injuries since 2014** — more than 100 cuts/lacerations, 29 broken bones/dislocations, 17 crushed hands/fingers, 9 head injuries, 8 amputations, 5 burns, 5 electrocutions, and 7 eye injuries — with ~400 occurring in years SpaceX failed to report to OSHA. The first death was **Lonnie LeBlanc, 38, in June 2014 at McGregor, Texas.**
- **Advertiser boycott / DealBook (29 Nov 2023) — PRESTIGE crisis.**
- **Musk Foundation disbursement shortfalls — PRESTIGE / philanthropy gap.**

## RECOMMENDATIONS (for game integration)

1. **Wire each incident to a specific meter delta and threshold, staged in time.** For William, model a two-phase loop: *aggregation* (papal banner → PRESTIGE↑, REGULATION↓; land-promises → GREED↑) then *crystallization* (Domesday → RENT locked in permanently). The "education corrodes prestige" mechanic pays off centuries later via the Grosvenor/Clark-Cummins epilogue — consider a "1,000 years later" post-credits stinger showing rent still flowing.
2. **Use the verbatim court/text material for the highest-fidelity cut scenes; use flagged reported speech for atmosphere.** Prioritize (a) Musk's DealBook and the Agrawal texts, (b) Wilkerson vs. Capone, (c) Casement witness testimony — all documentable — and clearly label Orderic's deathbed speech and Gusenberg's last words as reconstructed/apocryphal on screen.
3. **Model the "prestige shell" as a depletable buffer that repression can rupture.** Capone's soup kitchen and Leopold's museum/human-zoo are prestige investments; the Valentine's Massacre and the Casement/Harris photographs are the education spikes that puncture the shell and unlock regulation.
